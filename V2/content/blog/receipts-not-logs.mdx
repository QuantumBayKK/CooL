title: Why receipts, not logs

Every team running AI in production already has logs. So why another record?

Because a log answers "what does our database say happened?", and the question
that arrives after an incident is different: "can you prove what was live, and
that nobody changed the record since?"

## A log you keep

A log sits in a database your team controls. Whoever has write access can edit
it, and an edit looks exactly like the original. A deleted row leaves no gap.
Anyone checking it has to trust whoever runs it.

## A receipt anyone can check

A CooL receipt is built so that checking it does not require trusting us, or
you:

- The change is serialised deterministically and committed as salted hashes,
  so sensitive content never leaves your environment.
- The commitment is signed twice — ML-DSA-65 and Ed25519 — so it survives the
  failure of either scheme.
- The record is appended to an RFC 6962 transparency log. Inclusion proofs show
  it is there; consistency proofs show the log only ever grew.
- On confidential hardware, a TEE quote binds the measured code to the key that
  signed.

Edit one byte and binding and signature fail. Remove an entry and consistency
fails. And the verifier runs offline, on the checker's own machine.

## What a receipt does not do

It records what happened. It does not say whether the model's output was
correct, fair, safe or compliant — that judgement stays with the people
reading the evidence. A receipt makes sure they are reading the real thing.
